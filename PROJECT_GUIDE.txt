SPHEREx SkyBlink: Project & Website Guide
NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026: Planet X and SPHEREx
=====================================================

1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & PROBLEM STATEMENT
-----------------------------------------
• The Mystery of Planet X / Planet Nine:
  In the outer solar system beyond Neptune, several icy bodies orbit in strange, clustered alignments. Astronomers hypothesize a massive, undiscovered planet ("Planet X") is gravitationally pulling them. Because it orbits so far away, it reflects almost zero visible sunlight, making it invisible to standard optical telescopes.

• NASA's SPHEREx Mission:
  SPHEREx is a NASA space observatory designed to map the entire sky in 102 distinct near-infrared wavelengths every six months. Cold celestial objects glow faintly in the infrared, making SPHEREx ideal for detecting cold brown dwarfs, outer solar system bodies, and Planet X.

• Our Solution (SPHEREx SkyBlink):
  An interactive web platform that modernizes the historic "Blink Comparator" technique (used to discover Pluto in 1930) with multi-epoch infrared space data and 102-band spectroscopy, allowing anyone to detect and verify moving astronomical objects.


2. THE CORE SCIENTIFIC PRINCIPLE: BLINK COMPARATOR
---------------------------------------------------
• How it works:
  Take two photos of the same part of the sky taken months or years apart (Epoch 1 and Epoch 2).
  Rapidly alternate between them at 2 to 3 times per second (2-3 Hz).
  - Fixed distant stars stay completely motionless.
  - Moving objects (nearby stars, brown dwarfs, Planet X candidates) jump back and forth!

• History:
  In 1930, 24-year-old Clyde Tombaugh discovered Pluto using this exact mechanical technique with photographic glass plates. SkyBlink brings this into the browser with modern satellite data.


3. WALKTHROUGH OF EVERY FEATURE ON THE WEBSITE
-----------------------------------------------

A. Top Header
• Title & Challenge Badge: Shows "SPHEREx SkyBlink" and the NASA Space Apps 2026 challenge.
• Coordinate Readout: Displays current Right Ascension (RA) and Declination (Dec).
• Share Link: Generates a direct URL that preserves your target, zoom level, and settings.

B. Left Sidebar (Target Deck)
• Barnard's Star: Fastest-moving star in our sky (10.3 arcsec/year). Great demo for seeing clear motion.
• Luhman 16: Nearest binary brown dwarf system (6.5 light-years away). Faint in visible light, bright in infrared.
• Proxima Centauri: Nearest star to our Sun (4.2 light-years away), shows rapid motion across epochs.
• Planet X Candidate Field: Simulated outer solar system moving body blinking against distant stars.
• Variable & Transiting Stars: Objects that stay in one spot but pulse or dim dramatically over time.
• Custom RA/Dec Search: Enter any astronomical coordinates to query real NASA infrared cutouts.

C. Center Screen (The Sky Canvas)
• Infrared Space Viewport: Displays real mid-infrared data from space missions.
• NASA Viewfinder Reticles: Corner crosshairs framing the celestial target.
• Continuous Zoom (5' to 60' arcminutes): Smooth optical zoom from 1 degree wide down to a 5-arcminute close-up without loading pauses.
• Brightness & Contrast: Sliders to reveal faint objects hidden in space dust.
• Invert Colors: Switches between black sky and white background (standard method used by astronomers to spot faint contrast differences).
• Colormaps: Toggle between Grayscale, High-Contrast Heat, and Cold-IR palettes.
• Fullscreen Mode: Expands the sky view to fill the entire screen for planet hunting.

D. Blink & Analysis Controls (Under the Canvas)
• Play / Pause: Starts or stops the rapid alternating between Epoch 1 and Epoch 2.
• Blink Speed Slider: Adjusts animation speed from 0.5 Hz to 5.0 Hz (2-3 Hz is optimal).
• Manual Step: Lets you flip back and forth between Epoch 1 and Epoch 2 one frame at a time.
• Difference Image Mode: Calculates (Epoch 2 - Epoch 1). Static stars subtract to black (0 - 0 = 0). Anything that moved leaves a distinct bright/dark "dipole" pair, mathematically proving motion.

E. Bottom Titanium Drawer (SPHEREx 102-Band Inspector)
• 102-Band SED Graph: While normal cameras see 3 colors (RGB), SPHEREx splits infrared light into 102 narrow slices between 0.75 and 5.0 micrometers.
• Molecular Absorption Fingerprints:
  - Water Ice (H2O at 3.05 µm): Indicates icy surfaces on outer solar system bodies.
  - Methane (CH4 at 1.66 µm & 2.3 µm): Signature of cold gas giants, Planet X candidates, and T-type brown dwarfs.
  - Carbon Dioxide Ice (CO2 at 4.27 µm): Primordial cometary ice.
• NASA FITS Download: Direct link to download raw scientific FITS files.
• NASA WiseView Link: Direct button to open the target in WiseView for external verification.

F. Candidate Confirmation
• "Confirm Motion" / "Log Candidate": Calculates the angular motion vector and speed in milliarcseconds per year, celebrates with discovery confetti, and saves the candidate.

5. CHEAT SHEET: QUESTIONS JUDGES MIGHT ASK
-------------------------------------------
Q: Is this real NASA data?
A: Yes, real infrared sky cutouts from NASA's unWISE / NEOWISE missions, matching the infrared wavelengths of SPHEREx. The 102 spectral channels accurately model SPHEREx's linear variable filter spectrometers.

Q: Why a human blink comparator instead of AI?
A: AI algorithms suffer from high false-positive rates on cosmic ray hits and telescope artifacts. Major discoveries like Backyard Worlds: Planet 9 rely on human visual verification paired with difference imaging.

Q: How does this help find Planet X?
A: Planet X is too cold and far to reflect visible light, but glows in thermal infrared. By blinking multi-epoch infrared surveys and verifying methane absorption at 1.66 and 2.3 microns, SkyBlink provides the exact verification pipeline needed to confirm candidates.
